AutoMount Help

Network share manager for macOS

Getting Started

AutoMount makes it easy to manage and automatically reconnect network shares on your Mac. The app consists of two parts:

  • AutoMount — The main app where you add, edit, and manage your network shares.
  • AutoMount Background — A lightweight menu bar service that monitors your network and automatically remounts shares when connectivity is restored.

Add your network shares once, and AutoMount keeps them connected whenever your Mac is on the network.

Background Service & Login Item

AutoMount relies on a background login item called AutoMount Background to monitor your network and automatically remount shares. This service must be enabled for the app to function properly.

The background service:

  • Runs silently and checks the status of your configured shares.
  • Detects network changes and automatically remounts managed shares when connectivity is restored.
  • Periodically verifies that your managed shares remain connected.

Enabling the Login Item

The background service is configured as a Login Item in macOS. To enable it:

  1. Open System Settings → General → Login Items & Extensions.
  2. Find AutoMount in the list and toggle it on.
Important If the login item is disabled, AutoMount cannot monitor your network or automatically remount shares. You will see a warning in the main app window when this is the case.

Adding a Share

To add a new network share:

  1. Click the + button in the toolbar, or press ⌘K.
  2. Enter the server address (e.g., myserver.local) or select a previously used server.
  3. Enter the share path (the name of the shared folder on the server).
  4. Choose the protocol and configure options as needed.
  5. Click Save to add the share.

Mounting & Unmounting

Each share in the list displays a status indicator:

  • Green — The share is currently mounted and accessible.
  • Gray — The share is not mounted.
  • Orange — The share is in a transitional state (mounting or an error occurred).

To mount a share, select it and click Mount. To unmount, click the eject button or select Unmount.

Managed vs Unmanaged Shares

Shares can be either managed or unmanaged:

  • Managed — AutoMount will automatically remount these shares when your network connection is restored. The background service monitors for network changes and reconnects managed shares.
  • Unmanaged — These shares appear in the list for easy manual mounting but are not automatically reconnected.

Toggle the managed state by editing the share and changing its managed setting.

Auto-Remount & Background Service

The AutoMount Background service runs in your menu bar and provides automatic share management:

  • Network monitoring — Detects when your Mac connects to or disconnects from a network.
  • Automatic remount — When a network change is detected, the service waits for the network to stabilize (debounce period), then attempts to remount all managed shares.
  • Periodic remount — Periodically checks and remounts any managed shares that have become disconnected.
  • Volume tracking — Monitors macOS volume mount and unmount events to keep share status up to date.
Tip Enable "Show in menu bar" in Settings to see the AutoMount icon in your menu bar for quick access to your shares.

Settings

Open Settings from the app menu (⌘,) to configure:

  • Show in menu bar — Toggle the menu bar icon for the background service.
  • Network debounce — How long (in seconds) to wait after a network change before attempting to remount shares. A longer delay helps avoid repeated mount attempts on unstable connections. Default: a few seconds.
  • Periodic remount interval — How often (in minutes) the background service checks and remounts disconnected managed shares.
  • Clear previous servers — Removes the saved list of previously used server addresses from the auto-complete suggestions.

FAQ

Why isn't my share reconnecting automatically?

Make sure the share is set to managed. Only managed shares are automatically remounted by the background service. Also verify that the AutoMount Background service is running (check for its icon in the menu bar).

How do I remove a share?

Select the share in the list and press ⌫ (Delete), or right-click and choose Delete. The share will be unmounted if currently connected and removed from your list.

What protocols are supported?

AutoMount supports SMB and NFS protocols. SMB is recommended for most use cases as it offers the best cross-platform compatibility.

Can I use AutoMount with VPN connections?

Yes. When a VPN connects, AutoMount detects the network change and will attempt to remount your managed shares. You may want to increase the network debounce setting to allow time for the VPN to fully establish.

Where are my share configurations stored?

Share configurations are stored in the app group container shared between AutoMount and the background service. They persist across app restarts and updates.

Troubleshooting

If you encounter errors when mounting shares, here are common causes and solutions:

ErrorCauseSolution
Authentication failed Invalid username or password Verify your credentials. You may need to update saved passwords in Keychain Access.
Host not found The server address cannot be resolved Check the server address for typos. Ensure you are connected to the correct network and the server is online.
Connection timed out The server is unreachable Verify network connectivity. The server may be offline, or a firewall may be blocking the connection.
Share not found The share name does not exist on the server Double-check the share path. The share may have been renamed or removed on the server.
Connection refused The server is not accepting connections on the expected port Ensure the file sharing service is enabled on the server and the correct protocol is selected.
Already mounted The share is already connected The share is accessible. Check Finder for the mounted volume.

Shares fail to mount or mount hangs indefinitely

If a share appears to hang while mounting, or mount operations never complete, the macOS network authentication agents (NetAuthAgent or NetAuthSysAgent) may have become unresponsive. This is a known macOS issue that can occur after sleep/wake cycles or network changes.

Fix: Restart the network authentication agents

macOS will automatically restart these agents after they are killed. To do this:

  1. Open Terminal (search for it in Spotlight with ⌘Space, then type Terminal).
  2. Paste and run the following command:

sudo killall NetAuthAgent NetAuthSysAgent

You will be prompted to enter your Mac login password. The agents will restart automatically, and you can then try mounting your shares again.
